\section{Introduction}%
\label{sec:introduction}
%Each comment introduces 1-2 sentences 
% Context 
Knowledge graphs (KGs) play a vital role in enabling semantic interoperability,
integration, and querying. 
They allow systems to execute advanced semantic queries, facilitating the
integration and reasoning of the data at hand. 
The importance of KGs is further amplified by the growing adoption of large language
models (LLMs) alongside KGs for tasks like retrieval-augmented
generation~\cite{Xu2024RAGKGLinkedin}. 
However, many existing data infrastructures lack the capability to generate KGs
dynamically, and migrating legacy databases with diverse data formats to 
KG-based systems can be costly for organizations.
Unlocking the wealth of knowledge in these legacy systems require the generation 
of KGs from heterogeneous data efficiently. 
One such approach of generating KGs involves the usage of declarative mapping
languages~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}. 

Application of declarative mapping languages has gained traction in recent years across
diverse domains such as COVID research~\cite{Sakor2023KG4Covid}, railway
networks~\cite{rojas2021leveraging}, and incident management in ICT
systems~\cite{Tailhardat2024NORIA}. 
A plethora of mapping languages exist to support these practical applications. 
On one hand, query-based languages like SPARQL-Generate~\cite{lefrancois_eswc_2017} aim to
extend SPARQL for KG generation from heterogeneous data. 
On the other hand, dedicated mapping languages, such as the RDF Mapping Language
(RML)~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014}, use custom syntax. 
Furthermore, a single mapping language can have multiple implementations for
generating KGs. For example, RML has several
implementations~\cite{Freund2024FlexRML,Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph,ArenasGuerrero2022MorphKGCScalable,MinOo2022RMLStreamer,Scrocca2020Transport},
each offering distinct advantages for their own unique use cases (i.e.,
FlexRML~\cite{Freund2024FlexRML} for KG generation on IoT devices). 


% what’s the problem
State-of-the-art mapping engines exhibit varying operational semantics due to
the lack of well-defined formal foundation in the mapping languages they are
built upon. 
This absence of formalizations complicates verifying the correctness of the
mapping engines and studying the expressiveness of the mapping languages.
Additionally, it poses challenges in optimizing the mappings described by the
mapping languages.

% why it’s an interesting problem

% what’s my solution + paper outline
In this paper, we fill the gap in the lack of formalizations in 
mapping languages by providing mapping language-agnostic
formalizations with three contributions.
The first contribution is the introduction of formal definitions and the proposal
of a set of algebraic mapping operators.
These operators can be composed to formally define the mapping of
heterogeneous data to KG in a language-agnostic manner
(Sections~\ref{sec:data_model} and~\ref{sec:mapping_algebra}).
A language-agnostic formalization of mapping heterogeneous data to KG lays the
foundation for a unified mapping engine that can work with the user's preferred
mapping language. 
This approach ensures verified correctness and enables optimizations across
various mapping languages.
The second contribution demonstrates the expressiveness of our formalisms by
applying them to a specific mapping language. 
Specifically, we present an algorithm that translates RML documents into
expressions composed of the algebraic operators defined in our formalism
(Section~\ref{sec:expressiveness}). 
This algorithm shows that our formalism can represent every mapping definable
in RML, proving it to be at least as expressive as RML while also providing
formal semantics for RML. 
Lastly, as our final contribution, we introduce a few algebraic equivalences
to show the potential of algebraic expression rewriting and thus, enabling the
optimizations of mapping heterogeneous data to KG (Section~\ref{sec:equivalences}).
