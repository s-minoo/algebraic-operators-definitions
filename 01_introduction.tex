Knowledge graphs (KGs) have seen increased usage alongside large language
models (LLMs), enhancing LLMs with external knowledge for tasks such as
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{Xu2024RAGKGLinkedin}. 
The KGs required for LLM applications

may already exist or may need to be generated from existing structured or semi-structured 
(heterogeneous)
data sources such as tabular data in CSV format or nested data objects in JSON.
Naturally, empowering LLMs with the vast amount of existing heterogeneous
data enables the reuse of existing data-generating infrastructures, 
and eliminating the additional costs of setting up new KG-generating sources.
One approach to generate KGs from existing heterogeneous data sources is
through the usage of declarative mapping languages~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}.

Declarative mapping languages specify the mappings for generating KGs from
heterogeneous data based on a mapping document written in the respective
language. 
The RDF Mapping Language
(RML)~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014} is one example of
such a declarative mapping language.
Usage of RML, for generating KGs in RDF format from heterogeneous data sources,
has gained traction in recent years across diverse domains such as COVID
research~\cite{Sakor2023KG4Covid}, railway networks~\cite{rojas2021leveraging},
and incident management in ICT systems~\cite{Tailhardat2024NORIA}. 
Several
RML-based mapping engines have been developed to generate KGs from
heterogeneous data, each with its unique approach to execute the mapping
process (i.e. generating KG from existing heterogeneous
data~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}). 
For example, FlexRML~\cite{Freund2024FlexRML} focuses on the generation of KGs
for resource-constrained IoT devices, while
SDM-RDFizer~\cite{Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph} aims for an efficient and
scalable KG generation through novel optimization techniques and data
structures.
