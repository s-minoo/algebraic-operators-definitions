\section{Related Work}%
\label{sec:related_works}
{\color{red}
According to Van Assche et al.~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph},
declarative mapping languages can be categorized into two categories: 
i) \emph{dedicated languages} which extend existing mapping languages, 
such as RML~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014} extending R2RML~\cite{Seema2012R2RML}, 
or use custom syntaxes, and 
ii) \emph{repurposed languages} which repurpose existing non-mapping languages, 
such as query languages, to support KG construction.
}
{\color{blue}
We categorize mapping languages into two categories following the work
of Van Assche et al.~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph} 
as follows: 
i) \emph{dedicated languages} which are built specifically for the purpose of
KG construction, such as
RML~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014} and R2RML~\cite{Seema2012R2RML}, or use custom syntaxes, and 
ii) \emph{repurposed languages} which repurpose existing non-mapping languages, 
such as query languages, to support KG construction.
Do note that we adapted the original definition for the category on \emph{dedicated languages}
to also include R2RML which does not extend any mapping languages.
}
In this section, we briefly review existing works on formalizations of 
declarative mapping languages for KG construction. 

\paragraph{Formalizations in Repurposed Mapping Languages.}
To the best of our knowledge, only SPARQL-based mapping languages have defined
formal
semantics~\cite{lefrancois_eswc_2017,Lopes2011XSPARQL,Bischof:2012:XSPARQL,asprino2023knowledge}.
SPARQL-Anything~\cite{asprino2023knowledge} utilizes a two-step approach. 
First, the authors formalize the Façade-X
meta-model~\cite{Daga2021FacadeXOpinionated}, which is used to transform
heterogeneous data into an intermediate RDF graph describing the structure of
data with their values.
Lastly, they use SPARQL CONSTRUCT query on the Façade-X RDF graph for 
generating the KG, ensuring full compatibility with the formal semantics of SPARQL. 
SPARQL-Generate~\cite{lefrancois_eswc_2017} extends SPARQL 1.1 to support mapping
heterogeneous data to KG, thus, inheriting the formal semantics of SPARQL.
XSPARQL~\cite{Bischof:2012:XSPARQL} combines the semantics of XQuery and SPARQL
for mapping between XML and RDF data.
The authors extend their work to support mapping relational databases to RDF
data by utilizing the semantics of relational algebra~\cite{Lopes2011XSPARQL}. 
The aforementioned works rely on the semantics of the underlying non-mapping
languages. 
However, we argue that such reliance complicates the application of these
formalisms to dedicated mapping languages.  

\paragraph{Formalizations in Dedicated Mapping Languages.}
R2RML~\cite{Seema2012R2RML} is a dedicated mapping language for R2RML mappings,
which are RDF graphs for mapping relational databases to RDF KG.
%These mappings are usually presented using Turtle syntax.
R2RML has served as the basis for several mapping languages that extend its
scope from relational databases to structured or semi-structured
data~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}, such as
RML~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014}.
%, D2RML~\cite{Chortaras2018D2RML}, and xR2RML~\cite{michel2015translation}. 
Thus, it is important to consider the formalization studies on 
R2RML, as they can impact languages within the R2RML family.

Sequeda et al.~\cite{Sequeda2013R2RML} provide formalization to the semantics
of R2RML using Datalog.
The authors prove that the semantics of R2RML can be captured using 57 distinct
Datalog rules to generate RDF from relational databases. 
While this formalization is focused on 
%a single type of input data, namely a 
relational databases, our approach is designed to capture arbitrary types of 
heterogeneous data sources for mapping.
Furthermore, Datalog-based formalization adds complexity when implementing
compliant mapping engines due to the lack of control-flow description. 
In contrast, our approach clearly defines algebraic operators and
introduces control-flow, making it easier to adopt and implement a functional
mapping engine.
%D2RML and xR2ML do not provide any formal operational semantics for executing
%the mapping process described in their respective languages nor were there any
%studies conducted for formalizing these languages.
Besides R2RML, a few studies on formalization of RML have been published in 
recent years~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra,Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph}.
Iglesias et al.~\cite{Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph} provide formal
semantics to the process of KG generation through Horn clauses. 
However, their primary focus is optimizing the KG creation process for
efficiency, with formalizations as a by-product to support their optimization
techniques.
It would also be interesting to explore applying their formalization to mapping
languages beyond RML. 
An initial study provided formal semantics for the 
mappings in a language-agnostic manner through \emph{algebraic mapping
operators}~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra}.
This preliminary work does not cover all aspects of the mappings that can be
described using a dedicated mapping language like RML (i.e., joining multiple
data sources). 
Another limitation of this paper is that the definitions are only
\emph{semi-formal}; with some presented informally or relying on undefined concepts.
Thus, there is still a lack of study in providing language-agnostic, complete,
and well-defined semantics for mapping heterogeneous data to KG.
