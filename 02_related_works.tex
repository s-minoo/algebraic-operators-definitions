\subsection{Formalizations in Dedicated Mapping Languages}
\label{sub:dedicated_mapping_languages}
R2RML~\cite{Seema2012R2RML} is a dedicated mapping language for R2RML mappings,
which are RDF graphs for mapping relational databases to RDF KG.
These mappings are usually presented using Turtle syntax.
R2RML has been used as the basis for several mapping languages which extend its
scope from relational databases to structured or semi-structured
data~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}, such as
RML~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014}, D2RML~\cite{Chortaras2018D2RML}, and
xR2RML~\cite{michel2015translation}. 
Thus, it is important to consider the formalization studies conducted on 
R2RML which can have impact on the languages in the R2RML family.  

Sequeda et al.~\cite{Sequeda2013R2RML} provide formalization to the semantics
of R2RML using Datalog.
The authors prove that the semantics of R2RML can be captured using 57 distinct
Datalog rules to generate RDF from relational databases. 
While this formalization is focused on a single type of input data, namely a 
relational database, our approach is designed to capture arbitrary types of 
heterogeneous data sources for mapping.
Furthermore, Datalog-based formalization limits the accessibility for
developers aiming to implement mapping engines that comply with these
formalisms due to the lack of control-flow description. 
In contrast, our approach defines algebraic operators in a clear, and concise
manner, while also introducing control-flow to make it easier to adopt and implement a working mapping engine.

Besides R2RML, a few studies on formalization of RML have been published in 
recent years~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra,Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph}
Iglesias et al.~\cite{Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph} provide formal
semantics to the process of KG generation through Horn clauses. 
However, their primary focus is on optimizing the KG creation process for
efficiency, with the formalizations created as a side product to support their
novel optimization techniques.
Moreover, it would be interesting to explore the application of their
formalization to mapping languages beyond RML. 
 
An initial study explored the provision of formal semantics for the 
mapping process in a language-agnostic manner through \emph{algebraic mapping
operators}~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra}.
This preliminary work does not cover all aspects of the KG generation process that 
can be described using a dedicated mapping language like RML.
Notably, it does not provide semantics for joining multiple data sources. 
Another limitation of this paper is that its definitions are only
"semi-formal"; meaning that some definitions are either presented informally or rely on
concepts that are undefined.
Thus, there is still a lack of study in providing language-agnostic, complete,
and well-defined semantics to the mapping process of generating knowledge
graphs from heterogeneous data.
