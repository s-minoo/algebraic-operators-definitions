\section{Related Works}%
\label{sec:related_works}

According to Van Assche et al.~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}, 
declarative mapping languages can be further categorized into two categories: 
i) \emph{dedicated languages} which extend specifications or use custom
syntaxes, and ii) \emph{repurposed languages} which repurpose existing
specifications or query languages to support knowledge graph construction.
In this section, we briefly review existing works on formalizations of 
dedicated mapping languages for knowledge graph construction. 

We do not consider repurposed mapping languages in this work since they 
inherit the formalizations of the specifications or query languages on which
they are based upon, limiting them to the syntax of the parent languages and not 
applicable for formalizing a language-agnostic mapping process. 

\subsection{Formalizations in Dedicated Mapping Languages}
\label{sub:dedicated_mapping_languages}
R2RML~\cite{Seema2012R2RML} is a dedicated mapping language, using
Turtle-syntax, for generating knowledge graphs from relational databases. 
It has been used as the basis for a family of mapping languages which extends 
its scope from relational database to (semi)-structured data~\cite{VanAssche2022DeclarativeRDFgraph}, 
such as RML~\cite{dimou_ldow_2014}, D2RML~\cite{Chortaras2018D2RML}, and xR2RML~\cite{michel2015translation}. 
Thus, it is important to consider the formalization studies conducted on 
R2RML which can have impact on the languages in the R2RML family.  

Sequeda et al.~\cite{Sequeda2013R2RML} provided formalizations of the 
operational semantics of R2RML using Datalog.
They proved that the semantics of R2RML can be captured using 57 distinct 
Datalog rules to generate RDF from relational databases. 
However, since R2RML's scope is limited to relational databases, the same 
formalizations cannot be applied to other languages whose scope is much larger
than just relational databases.  

D2RML and xR2ML do not provide any formal operational semantics for executing
the mapping process described in their respective languages nor were there any
studies conducted for formalizing these languages.
In contrast, a few studies on formalization of RML have been published in 
recent years~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra,Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph}
Iglesias et al.~\cite{Iglesias2022ScalingKnowledgeGraph} focused on formalizing 
the optimization of executing the mapping process in a RML document.
The formalization relied on the syntax of RML to provide 
operational semantics such as using RML's concept of triples map to 
describe mapping assertions. 
Furthermore, the authors did not explore if it is possible to apply the same
formalizations to provide semantics for other mapping languages where the 
concept of triples maps do not exist. 

An initial study explored the provision of formal semantics for the 
mapping process in a language-agnostic manner through \emph{algebraic mapping
operators}~\cite{MinOo2023TowardsAlgebra}. 
However, this preliminary work does not provide a complete semantics for 
mapping processes, since it does not provide a way to capture the semantics of
joining multiple mapping processes which RML can describe. 
Thus, there is still a lack of study in providing language-agnostic, complete,
and well-defined semantics to the mapping process of generating knowledge
graphs from (semi)-structured data.
