\begin{abstract}
% Context (What is needed to understand the "need"?)
Mapping languages such as RML and R2RML describes the mapping process to
construct knowledge graphs from heterogeneous data sources. 
% Need (Why something needed to be done at all?)
Several implementations of these mapping languages exhibit 
operational semantics distinct from each other, due to the lack of formalizations of 
the mapping languages. 
This lack of formalizations has lead to discrepancies across the
implementations, both in the features supported and in the optimization
techniques.
% Task (What was undertaken to address the need? It’s here that you write ‘in this paper, we …’)
% Object (What the present document does or covers)
In this paper, we present the formal definitions of algebraic mapping operators that can
be composed to create executable mapping definitions, enabling the
construction of knowledge graphs from heterogeneous data sources. 
We also provide a translation algorithm for transforming RML documents into the 
mapping definitions based on algebraic mapping operators. 
% Findings (What the work done yielded or revealed)
This approach establishes a formal operational semantics for the RML mapping language, 
and consequently for R2RML, as RML is a superset of R2RML.
Furthermore, we provide a few examples where our algebraic mapping operators 
are more expressive than RML's semantics. %TODO: EXAMPLES NEEDED!!!
% Conclusion (What the findings mean for the audience)
% Perspectives (What the future holds, beyond this work)
The algebraic mapping operators offer a theoretical foundation for defining the
operational semantics of both current and future mapping languages,
promoting a more unified approach to constructing knowledge graphs from
heterogeneous data sources.
\keywords{Mapping Algebra \and Formalization \and RML \and Knowledge Graph Construction.}
\end{abstract}
